Search report

Search engines

Five search engines were selected for this work: Google, Yandex, Bing, DuckDuckGo and Yahoo! Google is one of the largest search engines in the world; it uses its own engine and provides the widest coverage of English- and Russian-language sources. Yandex is the leading Russian search engine, better adapted to the Russian-language segment of the Internet. Bing is Microsoft's search engine with its own ranking algorithms and result selection. DuckDuckGo is privacy-oriented and aggregates results from several sources. Yahoo! uses the Bing engine while keeping its own interface and ranking specifics.

Advanced search with the language parameter was used to refine the results.

Analysis

The query on the thesis topic deserves separate attention. In Russian, Google returned 12,400 results, Bing only 18, Yahoo! — “Not found”. This shows that the exact Russian wording of the topic is very narrow and almost never occurs on the Internet in this form. In English the picture is wider: Google — 1,240,000, Bing — 2,350, Yahoo! — 2,340. This confirms that there is more English-language material on the topic and that the English wording is closer to international terminology.

A similar situation is observed when searching for the supervisor's name. In Russian, Google showed 1,870 results, Bing and Yahoo! — 24 each. This means the supervisor's visibility in the information space is moderate: there are publications, manuals and mentions, but they are not widespread.

As for the key phrases, the picture is different. “Smart contracts” is a broad topic: Google gives 2,450,000 (RU) and 128,000,000 (EN). “Blockchain” is also broad: 5,670,000 (RU) and 892,000,000 (EN) in Google. “Digital assets” is narrower but still well represented: 3,210,000 (RU) and 156,000,000 (EN) in Google. Bing and Yahoo! return orders of magnitude fewer results, which is explained by their smaller indexes and different ranking algorithms. Google — best coverage and the largest results, especially for English-language queries. Yandex — the exact number of results is hidden, but the results contain relevant Russian-language scientific and technical sources. Bing — a moderate number of results, good for English-language queries but noticeably fewer Russian-language sources. DuckDuckGo — the number of results is hidden, the output is aggregated and useful for finding independent sources. Yahoo! — results are close to Bing, which confirms the use of a common search engine.

Comparison of the two measurements

In the repeated measurement of 21.11.2026, most search engines hid the exact number of results: Google and Yahoo! finally removed the counter from the interface, and Bing displayed it only for the thesis topic query. This is the only comparable value: 18 documents on 15.09.2026 versus 25,400 on 21.11.2026. Such growth means that over two months the materials on the topic were indexed much more widely (conference publications, digital libraries), and the topic is no longer “narrow” for Bing. The presence of relevant results was confirmed for all ten queries at both measurement points.

Conclusion

As a result of the work, an initial thematic search on the master's thesis topic was carried out using five search engines: Google, Yandex, Bing, DuckDuckGo and Yahoo!. Queries in Russian and English were formed, including the topic of the work, the supervisor's name and three key phrases. For each query, the number of documents found and full links with the search phrase were recorded. The analysis showed that Google provides the widest coverage. Yandex and DuckDuckGo do not display the exact number of results but give a relevant alternative output. The Russian wording of the topic is very narrow (Bing — 18, Yahoo! — 0), while the English one gives a much wider picture. The repeated measurement (21.11.2026) showed that search engines increasingly hide the exact number of results, but the results for all queries remain, and the topic coverage in Bing has grown significantly (18 → 25,400). The results obtained were used to form the abstract, library and links sections of the master's student's personal website.
